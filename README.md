# From consumption data to behavioural insights: estimating flexibility preferences  for demand-responsive energy communities
Dario Vezzali [^1], Haoqi Xie [^1], Stanislav Fedorov [^1] and Maria Elena Bruni [^1]

## Abstract
Energy communities play a crucial role in supporting the energy transition by enabling decentralized renewable energy generation and local energy management. However, unlocking their full potential requires effective demand-response mechanisms. This, in turn, depends on a deep understanding of the behavioural flexibility of participating actors to design demand-side solutions that extend beyond the simple use of price incentives.  To this end, this paper develops an activity-based modelling framework for the energy sector and applies it to estimate household-level flexibility parameters for appliance load scheduling within energy communities.  Using consumption data from the Plegma dataset, a high-frequency electricity measurement dataset collected from the Athenian energy community in the municipality of Attica, Greece, we reconstruct the daily load schedules of 13 households for four appliances (air conditioner, boiler, dishwasher, and washing machine/washer dryer), over a period of 15 months (between July 2022 and September 2023). For each household, we generate a sample of alternative schedules through an adaptation of the OASIS framework, originally developed for activity-based modelling in transportation, to the energy sector. The results demonstrate the potential of activity-based modelling to capture behavioural flexibility at the household level and provide a foundation for the development of data-driven demand-side solutions, as well as incentive mechanisms for demand-responsive energy communities.

## Keywords
Energy communities; Activity-based modelling; Behavioural flexibility; Demand response; Load scheduling.

## Highlights
- Behavioural flexibility in energy communities is investigated.
- An activity-based modelling framework is adapted for appliance load scheduling.
- Household-level flexibility parameters are estimated from real consumption data.
- Households show heterogeneous scheduling preferences and flexibility disutilities.
- Estimated parameters support fair, personalised demand-response incentive design.
